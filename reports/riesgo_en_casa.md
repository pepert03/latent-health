# Cardiovascular risk from what is measured at home

Generated by `scripts/fit_outcomes.py`. Data: NHANES 1999-2014 linked to the US death registry (follow-up through December 2019). Of 42,305 adults, the 34,443 with all the home data are used: in NHANES a missing value usually belongs to someone who could not be examined, who is frailer, and the model must not exploit that. Outcome: **death from heart disease or stroke within the next 10 years**.

Evaluation: 12,710 people aged 40 or older from the 1999-2008 cycles (all with 10 years of follow-up), 701 cardiovascular deaths (5.5%). Five-fold cross-validation: each person is predicted by a model that did not see them. The 95% bootstrap interval in parentheses.

- **AUC**: probability that the model gives higher risk to someone who dies than to someone who does not (0.5 is chance; 1, perfect).
- **AUC within each cycle**: the same, computed cycle by cycle. Rules out the model being right by recognizing the era rather than the patient.
- **Deaths in the top 20% risk**: of all deaths, how many fall in the fifth the model flags.

## What each thing the patient does adds

"Clinical history" is what is already on record without ordering anything: diagnoses, medications, smoking, weight and waist. The "questionnaire" is five questions: self-rated health, shortness of breath walking uphill, difficulty walking 400 meters and climbing 10 steps, and hospital admissions in the last year.

## The model in use: it works with whatever is available

In the product there is a single model, trained with blocks of data randomly dropped (questionnaire, weight and waist, BP cuff, urine). If the patient has not yet provided something, it estimates from the rest. In the held-out fifth (2521 people, 146 deaths), vs the model trained only for that case:

## Limits

- **US population.** Patient ranking should carry over; absolute risk should not (fewer people die of heart disease in Spain). Before giving percentages to a Spanish patient, recalibration is needed.
- **Deaths only.** The public file has no non-fatal heart attacks or strokes, which is what SCORE2 aims to predict. The comparison is about who dies, not who gets sick.
- **SCORE2 is not meant for people who already have cardiovascular disease.** That is why the column without prior disease is also given; it is the fair comparison.
- **Questionnaire administered by an interviewer**, not by the patient on their phone; and blood pressure and pulse taken by trained staff, not at home.
- **The urine is a single random sample measured in the lab.** The strip row simulates its reading with an assumed error.
- In the public mortality files, the time or cause of some records is replaced with synthetic values to protect identity.
