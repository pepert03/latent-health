# eGFR trend: simulation check

Generated by `scripts/validate_trend.py`. **This is not a validation with patients**: NHANES has no repeated lab tests. These are 3000 simulated patients built on the model's own assumptions (slope, creatinine noise and treatment effects taken from the literature), with one lab test per year.

## Detecting decline by number of lab tests

- **Interval**: how often the true slope (the initial one) falls inside the filter's 80% interval. The slope drifts a little over the years, so somewhat below 80% is expected.
- **Caught**: of those losing more than 5 per year, how many it flags.
- **False alarm**: of those losing less than 2 per year, how many it flags.
- **Filter, alert**: flags when P(slope < -3) ≥ 80% (what shows up as a finding on the worklist).
- **Filter, warning**: flags when P(slope < -3) ≥ 50% (doubtful trend or worse: bring the next lab test forward).
- **Rule of thumb**: flag if the mean drop between the first and last lab test exceeds 5 per year.

| Annual lab tests | 80% interval | Alert: caught | Alert: false alarm | Warning: caught | Warning: false alarm | Rule of thumb: caught | Rule of thumb: false alarm |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 82% | 1% | 0.0% | 6% | 0.3% | 60% | 24.3% |
| 3 | 84% | 17% | 0.2% | 38% | 1.1% | 62% | 11.2% |
| 4 | 83% | 45% | 0.2% | 72% | 1.2% | 79% | 3.5% |
| 5 | 84% | 71% | 0.2% | 90% | 1.5% | 78% | 1.5% |
| 6 | 84% | 88% | 0.3% | 96% | 1.9% | 85% | 0.7% |

## Starting an SGLT2 inhibitor

2316 stable patients (losing less than 2 per year) who start an SGLT2 inhibitor between the second and third of four annual lab tests. The drug lowers eGFR by about 3 points at first.

| | Flagged as declining |
|---|---|
| Without telling the filter that treatment started | 1.1% |
| With treatment as a known input | 0.2% |

## Limits

- All parameters are assumptions. They must be fitted with repeated primary care lab tests.
- The model does not account for acute kidney injury: it would read a sudden, reversible drop as decline.
- Creatinine depends on muscle mass; a large weight change moves estimated eGFR without the kidney changing.
