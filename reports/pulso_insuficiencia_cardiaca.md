# Heart failure signal in the resting pulse

Generated by `scripts/fit_pulse_hf.py`. Data: two open PhysioNet databases with 24-hour beat times from 29 people with heart failure ([chf2db](https://physionet.org/content/chf2db/1.0.0/), NYHA classes I to III, median age 58) and 54 in normal sinus rhythm ([nsr2db](https://physionet.org/content/nsr2db/1.0.0/), median age 65). They only contain the time of each beat, which is exactly what a pulse sensor provides.

From each person, 48 segments spread across the recording are taken, as individual pulse measurements. The model uses the 17 irregularity measures from the atrial fibrillation detector plus five more (rate, absolute variability, and acceleration and deceleration capacity). Five-fold cross-validation by person. It does not use age: here the healthy people are older than the patients, so age cannot explain the difference.

## Results

| Input | AUC, one measurement | Sensitivity (90% specificity) | AUC, seven measurements | Sensitivity, seven |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| One 1-min measurement | 0.823 | 61% | 0.893 | 82% |
| One 2-min measurement | 0.829 | 65% | 0.888 | 82% |
| One 5-min measurement | 0.835 | 68% | 0.886 | 82% |
| 2 min, beats degraded like a camera's (simulated) | 0.813 | 59% | 0.890 | 77% |
| 2 min, trained on ECG and used with camera (simulated) | 0.684 | 24% | 0.785 | 40% |
| 2 min, heart rate only | 0.705 | 30% | 0.752 | 48% |
| 2 min, rate and variability (SDNN) | 0.709 | 31% | 0.785 | 50% |

- **Repeating the measurement helps**: a single stretch of fast, flat pulse says little; the same thing every day says a lot.
- **The camera rows are simulated** (20 ms of jitter and 3% of beats dropped).
- Classic variability (SDNN) does not separate the groups in short segments: the patients' ectopic beats inflate it. What separates them is the rate, the lack of decelerations and the pattern of beat-to-beat changes.

## Check in Spanish patients (MUSIC)

MUSIC does not include the beats, but it does include the 24-hour Holter summary of its patients in sinus rhythm. The same summary is computed in the two PhysioNet databases, a rule is trained on four numbers (mean interval, SDNN, RMSSD and pNN50) and applied to the Spanish patients. In the PhysioNet databases that rule has AUC 0.890 and flags 86% of patients at 90% specificity.

| Group | People | Mean interval (ms) | 24-h SDNN (ms) | RMSSD (ms) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Healthy (nsr2db) | 54 | 780 | 139 | 28 |
| Heart failure, 1990s (chf2db) | 29 | 689 | 78 | 39 |
| Heart failure, Spain 2003-2004 (MUSIC) | 587 | 857 | 106 | 26 |

| Spanish patients with heart failure | Patients | Flagged by the rule |
|---|---|---|
| All (sinus rhythm, with analyzed Holter) | 587 | 22% |
| On a beta-blocker | 415 | 19% |
| Not on a beta-blocker | 172 | 30% |
| Functional class II | 487 | 20% |
| Functional class III | 100 | 36% |
| Ejection fraction ≤ 40% | 428 | 23% |
| Ejection fraction > 40% | 157 | 20% |
| Died during follow-up | 143 | 33% |
| Remained alive | 444 | 19% |

The Spanish patients, mostly treated with beta-blockers, have a slower and more variable pulse than those from the 1990s: the pulse rule flags them less often. This is the main caveat of this detector.

## Limits

- **83 people.** Confidence intervals are wide and any figure can move several points.
- **The controls are healthy.** In primary care the real comparison is with older people with hypertension, diabetes or COPD, who also have a less variable pulse. Real specificity will be lower.
- **The patients are from the 1990s**, before beta-blockers were standard treatment. The MUSIC check quantifies this.
- **The segments come from a 24-hour recording**, mixing sleep and activity; they are not seated resting measurements.
- For all these reasons the result enters the patient record with its likelihood ratio capped at what was seen in the Spanish patients: at most it multiplies the odds by 2.2 or leaves them at 0.86 times. It never decides alone.
