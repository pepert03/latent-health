# Heart failure: what can be known from home

Generated by `scripts/fit_heart_home.py`. Data: NHANES 1999-2004, 10,174 adults with measured NT-proBNP and all the home data, with mortality linked through 2019. Five-fold cross-validation; the 95% interval in parentheses.

NT-proBNP is the lab test primary care uses to rule out heart failure: below 125 pg/ml it is very unlikely. It cannot be measured at home.

## Who will have NT-proBNP above 125 pg/ml?

20.5% of the 9930 people without diagnosed heart failure do, and 47.9% of the 3258 aged 60 or older.

## Limits

- NHANES has no echocardiogram or confirmed heart failure diagnosis: what is predicted is a lab test and death from heart disease (which includes heart attack), not heart failure itself.
- Sera from 1999-2004 and an interviewer-administered questionnaire.
- The most specific symptoms are missing (ankle swelling, sleeping propped up, rapid weight gain); NHANES does not ask about them, but the patient can report them.
