# Low ejection fraction from single-lead ECG and heart sound

Generated by `scripts/fit_ejection.py`. Data: [Zenodo 16934966](https://zenodo.org/records/16934966) (CC-BY 4.0), from the paper by Ma et al. in *Patterns* (2026). 500 people from Tongji Hospital in Wuhan, 60 with ejection fraction of 40% or less measured by echocardiogram (and 107 below 50%), and 120 from a second hospital (5 with low ejection fraction). Each person has four 30-second recordings, one per auscultation site, with an Eko DUO: a handheld device that records a single-lead ECG and heart sound at the same time.

This is the project's first dataset with **echocardiogram as ground truth and people without the disease** from the same clinic. Those without low ejection fraction are not healthy: they may have valve disease, arrhythmias or cardiomyopathies.

## What is measured

- **Pulse** (22 measures): beat-to-beat intervals. This is what a phone camera, a watch or a pulse oximeter provides.
- **ECG** (13 measures and the averaged beat at 32 points): QRS width, voltage and slope, T wave, ST segment.
- **Sound** (19 measures and the averaged sound in 16 segments across three bands): the phonocardiogram averaged beat by beat using the QRS as clock. It gives the delay of the first heart sound, systole duration, the relative strength of the two sounds and the energy between and after them.

Adding the full averaged beat and sound to the summary measures raises AUC by about two hundredths: the named measures capture almost all of the signal, but not all.

One model per level (shallow gradient-boosted trees). Cross-validation uses the five per-person folds published by the authors. A person's score is the mean of their four recordings.

| What is used | AUC (four recordings) | 95% interval | AUC with a single recording | Sensitivity at 90% specificity | Sensitivity at 80% specificity | AUC with a linear model |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Heart rate only | 0.682 | 0.60-0.76 |  | 32% | 58% |  |
| Pulse: beat-to-beat intervals | 0.682 | 0.60-0.76 | 0.660 | 35% | 48% | 0.675 |
| Single-lead ECG | 0.883 | 0.83-0.93 | 0.826 | 68% | 80% | 0.851 |
| ECG and heart sound | 0.911 | 0.87-0.94 | 0.867 | 70% | 83% | 0.866 |

- ECG adds 0.202 AUC to pulse (95% interval: 0.142 to 0.266).
- Sound adds 0.027 to ECG (-0.008 to 0.063).
- With ECG and sound, the score tracks the measured ejection fraction (rank correlation -0.46).
- Recordings discarded for lacking 8 valid beats: 0 of 2480.

## What a positive result means

With the cross-validation sensitivity (70%) and specificity (90%), depending on who is tested:

| Who | Flagged | Of those flagged, with low EF | Of those not flagged, with low EF |
|---|---|---|---|
| Over 65 without symptoms (2%) | 11% | 13% | 0.7% |
| Long-standing diabetes or hypertension (5%) | 13% | 27% | 1.7% |
| Visit for breathlessness or swelling (12%) | 17% | 49% | 4.3% |

The prevalences are orders of magnitude, not project data. It is mainly useful for ruling out and for ordering the echocardiogram waiting list, not for diagnosis.

## Limits

- **Patients from a Chinese hospital**, not Spanish primary care. The authors do not publish age or sex per person, so it cannot be adjusted or checked by subgroup.
- **Few cases**: 60 in the main set and 5 in the second hospital.
- **Reduced ejection fraction only.** Half of heart failure has preserved ejection fraction, and here it counts as "without the disease".
- **The device was used by healthcare staff** on the chest. It is unknown how the recording turns out if the patient does it, or with a phone microphone. The pulse level can be taken with the camera, but here it comes from the ECG.
- **Not compared with the authors' model** (a deep network on the full signal): the paper could not be read. These measures are deliberately simple and explainable.
