# Undiagnosed diabetes: home glucometer vs FINDRISC

Generated by `scripts/fit_diabetes_home.py`. Data: NHANES 2005-2018, 27,300 adults without diagnosed diabetes or treatment and with complete data. Reference: HbA1c ≥ 6.5%, present in 865 (3.2%). Five-fold cross-validation; the 95% interval in parentheses.

Glucose is the lab value with the error of a home glucometer (ISO 15197 standard: 7.5% deviation). Half of the people (50%) had fasted for 8 hours or more; the model also receives how many hours since they last ate.

## The two-step pathway: who gets their glucose measured

In the product, capillary glucose is not measured in everyone. It is measured in those whose clinical history leaves a probability of hidden diabetes of 2% or more, and then a lab test is ordered for those who, with their glucose, are still at 2% or more. Since one measurement is enough, it is done at the nearest pharmacy or with the patient's own glucometer, if they have one. Percentages are over all people without a diagnosis.

## One measurement or several?

- **All figures in this report use a single measurement per person.**
- With that measurement, the system tells 58% of those who take it that they are done; the rest are asked for a lab test.
- **Repeating to average out device error adds almost nothing.** With an error-free glucometer, which is the ceiling of repeating many times, AUC goes from 0.920 to 0.931 (+0.011; interval 0.007 to 0.015).
- **What cannot be measured here** is whether repeating it on different days adds more, because a person's glucose changes from day to day and NHANES has only one per person.
- In the product, a fasting glucose of 126 mg/dl or more is repeated on another day: with two days it shows up as a finding, which is what the diagnostic criterion requires.

## Limits

- **It is not a new finding that glucose detects diabetes**: fasting glucose is one of its diagnostic criteria. What is measured here is how much is gained over what is used today to screen without a lab test.
- **Lab glucose with simulated error**, not real home glucometer readings. Capillary and venous blood differ somewhat after eating.
- **A single measurement per person.** These data cannot show how much repeating it over several days helps.
- **The reference is a single HbA1c.** Diagnosis requires confirming it.
- FINDRISC without the fruit and vegetable question, which NHANES lacks (worth 1 point out of 26).
- US population.
