# Heart failure in Spanish patients: what can be seen from home

Generated by `scripts/validate_music.py`. Data: clinical table of [MUSIC](https://physionet.org/content/music-sudden-cardiac-death/1.0.1/) (PhysioNet, ODbL license): 992 outpatients with symptomatic heart failure from eight Spanish university hospitals (Murcia, Las Palmas, Madrid, Tarragona, Santiago, Barcelona, Mallorca and Seville), enrolled in 2003-2004 and followed for a median of 4.9 years. 266 died: 111 from pump failure, 94 from sudden death and 61 from non-cardiac causes.

Median age 67 years, 72% men, 22% in functional class III, 72% with ejection fraction of 40% or less, 68% on a beta-blocker. Only 8% had NT-proBNP below 125 ng/l, the threshold used today to rule out the disease.

**Everyone has the disease**, so this cohort cannot be used to detect it. It shows how much of its severity can be seen with what the patient can measure at home, and lets us check in Spanish patients what was learned from other data.

## 1. Who dies within 3 years?

Of 962 patients with enough follow-up, 221 died (23%). Five-fold cross-validation. There are four home measurements: how easily they get tired (functional class), weight, blood pressure and pulse (rate and whether it is irregular).

## Limits

- **Patients from hospital heart failure clinics, 2003-2004.** Today they are treated with drugs that did not exist then (sacubitril-valsartan, SGLT2 inhibitors), and primary care patients are older and more often have preserved ejection fraction.
- **The "home measurements" were taken by healthcare staff** at the visit. Functional class was assigned by a cardiologist; at home it would come from questions and be less precise.
- **This is prognosis, not diagnosis.** It says whom to watch more closely among those already diagnosed.
- It is not compared with the study's published risk score (which needs echocardiogram, Holter and lab tests); the last row of the first table serves as the reference for "everything the hospital measured".
